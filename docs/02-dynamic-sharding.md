# Dynamic sharding: splits, merges, and straddler settlement

Hyperscale's shard topology is elastic. A shard whose state grows past a threshold **splits** into two children; two sibling shards whose combined state shrinks below a threshold **merge** back into their parent. Both happen at runtime, orchestrated by the beacon, without halting the network — and without breaking atomicity for cross-shard transactions in flight across the boundary, because no cut falls under a yes.

Three structural choices make this tractable, and everything else in this document is machinery built on them:

1. **A shard is a subtree.** State lives in one global binary JMT; a shard is a prefix subtree of it ([03-state-and-sync.md](03-state-and-sync.md)). A split hands each child one of the parent root's two children; a merge composes the parent root back as `hash_internal(left, right)`. Resharding is a tree operation with a one-hash continuity proof, not a data migration.
2. **The boundary is a block, and it falls on a quiet shard.** A reshaping shard terminates at a definite **terminal block**, fixed by epoch schedule. The beacon fixes that schedule only once the shard has drained: core intake toward it pauses, and the shard asserts on chain that no row of it still hears a sibling. The drain has a deadline counted in beacon epochs, so the boundary stays a function of committed facts rather than message timing, and a shard that cannot quiet by it has its reshape cancelled, never forced. Transactions straddling the boundary are settled by rules over frozen chain content (§4).
3. **Everything derives from committed facts.** Cohort draws are seeded by beacon randomness; readiness is witnessed on-chain; successor genesis blocks are pure functions of the predecessor's terminal block and the scheduled cut, and fail closed when the store does not hold the subtree the genesis names. No step depends on message timing or operator input.

Main code homes: reshape admission/gating in `crates/beacon` (the fold's reshape module, over `PendingReshape` in `crates/types`), the flip and duty machinery in `crates/node` (`ReshapeOrchestrator`, split/merge flip, observer/keeper bootstrap), settlement in the `crates/types` settled-set predicate, fetched and served from `crates/node`, and checked by the vote fence in `crates/shard` and the finalize gate in `crates/execution`, thresholds in `crates/types` (`ReshapeThresholds`).

---

## 1. Triggers: load-driven, proof-carrying

A shard proposes its own resharding. When a shard's committed substate byte total crosses `ReshapeThresholds::split_bytes`, or the beacon's mean of its block fullness crosses `split_fullness`, its proposer includes a `ReshapeTrigger::Split` assertion in the block manifest. The assertion is a *claim about committed state*, and every replica validates it against its own byte accounting before voting — a Byzantine proposer cannot conjure a split. Merge triggers work the same way against much lower thresholds on both measures (`merge_bytes` and `merge_fullness`, each derived from its split threshold); the wide hysteresis gap between a fresh split child's size and the merge threshold prevents split/merge oscillation. Reshaping is disabled unless the thresholds are explicitly configured. Governance can move them by on-chain parameter vote, which is also how operationally driven merges are effected.

One place the predicate is deliberately out of play: across a pending halt recovery, a block whose ancestry crosses the sync-admitted halted suffix carries no assertion, and no replica requires one. The suffix is QC-attested but never locally executed by the fresh committee, so its byte total is unknowable until it commits — a commit that needs the very blocks being gated. Every member of the only committee that can vote there synced the same suffix, so the omission is agreed on both sides. Once the suffix commits and the frontier reconciles from storage, ordinary accounting resumes; an over-threshold shard simply asserts on a later block.

Triggers must be re-asserted; a trigger silent for `RESHAPE_TRIGGER_TTL_EPOCHS` lapses. The beacon tracks each shard's pending reshape in a `PendingReshape` record — `Split { cohort, cohort_seed, ... }` or `Merge { halves, keepers, ... }` — inside `BeaconState`.

## 2. The split lifecycle

**Admission and cohort draw.** On folding a split trigger (gated on the free validator pool, less the recovery reserve below, being deep enough to staff it), the beacon draws a committee-sized **observer cohort** from the pool, seeded by `cohort_seed` — beacon randomness frozen at admission. The shuffled cohort is halved: the first half is assigned to the left child, the remainder to the right. Observers get status `Observing` and start work immediately. Because the seed is frozen, a lapsed-then-reasserted split re-derives the *identical* cohort — an observer's sync target never moves under it.

**An id is not taken up over its previous life.** A reshape seats chains under ids that may have run before: a merge reforms the parent a split once ended, and a split forms children a merge once ended. While the terminal boundary record of the earlier chain stands in beacon state, the fold admits no reshape that would seat a new chain under that id (INV-RESHAPE-12): a split trigger is refused on either child id, a merge trigger on the parent id. A refused trigger is simply not counted. No cohort is drawn, no keeper is pinned and no window shows the asserting shard as pending, so its counterparts keep settling against it as against any running shard. Nothing is lost by the refusal either: a shard asserts its trigger afresh in every window its load holds, and the first Normal fold after the one that drops the record admits it.

The record stands until the earlier chain's successors are live and then through the terminal-evidence window, and every window that does not run the id projects it, so a shard still awaiting the earlier chain reads that chain's terminal evidence and never a different chain's state under the same id. The first Normal fold at or past `TERMINAL_EVIDENCE_EPOCHS + 2` epochs after the record's handoff stamp drops it; a Skip fold drops no record. By then the evidence has read unreadable at every anchor past its expiry. The trigger that fold carries was read while the record stood, so the next Normal fold is the first to admit; the cut lands at least a window after admission and the id runs a window after the cut. Three whole windows at the least, and one more for each fold the cohort takes to sync, therefore pass in which every replica reads the id gone and unreadable before any window runs it again. A shard that anchors a block in any of them reads its sibling gone there, not the answer of a chain that never held its transaction. Nothing rests on a block landing in the gap. Every boundary record names the epoch its chain formed at, whose window is the first to run it, and a member row committed before that window awaited an earlier chain under the id. To such a row the id reads gone at every anchor, whatever chain the window runs or records under it: only an accept of the earlier chain, read before the later chain formed, still counts. A block naming such a row in a reading of the later chain is refused, as is one naming a row in a reading anchored where the window shows no formation under the id, so a row hears only the chain it awaited and no later reading displaces that accept. A core transaction whose block is classified under one life of a shard it awaits and anchored in another is refused, so every row is committed in the life of each shard it awaits. The produced mark, the fullness mean and the recovery history kept under the id leave beacon state with the record, so a reformed shard inherits none of them: it reads live only once it has produced, and starts idle.

An id clear at admission stays clear until the reshape seats it. A terminal mark is only ever stamped on the live leaf a pending reshape ends; an id a pending reshape will seat is not a leaf, and no second reshape is admitted over the leaves the first involves. A record whose handoff never stamps holds its id for as long as it stands.

**Preparation (make-before-break).** While the parent keeps producing blocks, each observer snap-syncs its assigned child's subtree: a verified range-fetch of the child's key span against the beacon-anchored state root, followed by tailing the parent chain and applying each new block's child-prefix writes ([03-state-and-sync.md](03-state-and-sync.md) §5). When caught up, the observer emits a `ReshapeReady` witness — an on-chain, merkle-proven fact. The observer keeps following after that, because the follow is also how it will recognise the parent's terminal crossing as it passes and take over from it.

**The readiness gate.** The beacon's fold latches and schedules the split only when **both** children can seat a live committee: each child's prospective membership — its half of the parent's current committee (assigned deterministically from beacon randomness) plus its ready cohort seats — must reach 2f+1 of the committee target (INV-RESHAPE-1). Parent members carry their readiness and placement epoch across the flip; losing placement history was an observed liveness bug — it strands committees. Until both halves pass, nothing changes. If readiness doesn't arrive within `RESHAPE_READY_TTL_EPOCHS`, the reshape is abandoned and the cohort returns to the pool.

**The drain latch.** The first fold whose readiness gate passes does not schedule the cut. It latches the record's drain, setting `Admission.drain_by` to `reshape_drain_epochs()` past the fold, and schedules nothing in that fold. The latch is set once and clears only with its record, so once a window shows a record draining, every later window shows it until the cut or the cancel. Each window carries the latched shards in its `draining` map — a split's target, or each child of a merge, keyed to the record's admission epoch — and a shard is *reshaping* at an anchor whose frozen or lookahead window names it there or ends at its cut (`TopologySchedule::reshaping_at`). A window a replica does not hold reads as reshaping, so a proposer and a voter cannot disagree on it. While a shard is reshaping no core transaction toward or from it is admitted, the mempool passes over it, and a core member committed beside it is not run, so it gains no shared row in flight and a quiet shard stays quiet. A row already in flight states or ends by its stating window's close, its deadline plus `STATING_GRACE`, and a stated row then accepts or refuses on what its siblings say, so with every sibling readable, and unless a tick still owes its determined half ([04-atomic-commitment.md](04-atomic-commitment.md) §8), the drain lasts at most `RESHAPE_DRAIN_SPAN` (a validity range, plus the finalization delay, plus the grace: 444 s) from the first paused window. Intake stays open through cohort sync and pauses only from the latch, so a reshape with no fault pauses it for that span and the few folds around it.

**The quiet assertion.** A draining shard asserts its reshape once in each epoch whatever its load, except from a parent in a recovery's suffix band, through the trigger's `settling` field: the admission epoch its window names, and whether its rows are quiet (`MemberIndex::quiet`: no shared row in flight, no stated row and no accepted row). The assertion is read outside the byte count, so neither a suffix band's missing count nor thresholds voted to `DISABLED` cancel a draining reshape, and a voter holds it to equality off the parent's rows, holding its vote until it can rebuild them. The field is part of the witness leaf, so a flip to quiet asserts again within the epoch. The beacon folds a settling assertion only into a standing, latched record whose admission it names: it refreshes the record, re-staffs a lapsed cohort from the frozen seed, and raises the record's quiet bit, which then stays up until the record clears. An assertion naming another admission, from a stale anchor or a cancelled record, sets nothing. A witness leaf folds only under a proof against a boundary header, which lies at or below the attested frontier a halt recovery keeps, so a recovery never loses the block that asserted quiet.

**Scheduling the cut.** The beacon schedules the cut only at a fold where the record is latched, its readiness gate passes, and its quiet bit is up — for a merge, both children's. That fold does not reshape the trie. It stamps a cut — always on a *later* window than the one it opens (INV-RESHAPE-9) — onto the retained reshape record and onto the parent's boundary record, and stops there. The lead is what makes the cut knowable in advance: a proposer decides whether its header must carry the terminal-window verdicts by reading its own window's promotion-frozen projection, and a cut named for the window that fold opens would arrive after the proposers who needed it. One window is the minimum that suffices; more would only add latency.

Because the cut is published to proposers the moment it is stamped, it cannot be taken back (INV-RESHAPE-10). Two deadlines govern everything before it, and both cancel rather than force: the readiness TTL cancels an unlatched record, and `drain_by` cancels a latched one still unscheduled at it, quiet or not. `reshape_drain_epochs()` is `3 + ⌈RESHAPE_DRAIN_SPAN / epoch_duration⌉`, counted from the configured epoch length: one window for the latch to publish, the drain, one fold for the quiet assertion to ride a crossing and fold, and one because the cancel runs before that fold's schedulers. It is 5 at 300 s epochs and 18 at 30 s, and the deadline leaves room for a quiet assertion to fold the fold before `drain_by`; the cut is scheduled then if the readiness gate still passes. A cancel cuts nothing, returns the cohort, and lifts the pause a window later; a later admission needs an assertion on load. Once the cut is stamped, neither deadline applies; a cohort that degrades afterwards surfaces as a stalled handoff, not a retracted cut. The carve the gate approved is frozen onto the record with it, since there is no second gate to approve a different one.

**Execution.** The fold *at* the cut consumes the record: the parent's lookahead committee entry is replaced by the two children's committees, and the children receive placeholder boundary records (zero block hash) so they cannot yet be mistaken for snap-sync anchors. A member jailed or withdrawn since the carve was frozen is dropped rather than moved, and the child seats short until `top_up_committees` backfills it from the pool past the recovery reserve, which waits while the reserve is at least the pool.

**Recovery priority.** A halted or fork-flagged shard's redraw outranks every reshape draw. Its *recovery demand* (`BeaconState::recovery_demand`) is every fork-flagged shard and every halted shard the lookahead seats, less one that never produced (it has no attested anchor to seat against) and one whose cut applies in this fold (the apply unseats it). `recover_committees` runs between the readiness timeout and the shuffle, so a cohort a cancel returned in this fold reaches the recovery before any shuffle entrant draws, and it runs ahead of the reshape apply and schedule folds. Split admission and cohort re-staff draw only from the pool left past `shard_size` for each shard of the demand (`drawable_pool_len`), reading the demand a fold ahead because the witness fold runs before the miss count moves, and top-up draws only past the same reserve. The shuffle entrant is not gated: a rotation's victim returns its seat, and a gate would stop rotation on every shard during any recovery the pool cannot fund. The departure refill is not gated either: a committee losing a seat to a jail or a withdrawal outranks a recovery. A cohort admitted before its sibling entered demand keeps its seats until its own cancel, so it delays that recovery by at most `RESHAPE_READY_TTL_EPOCHS` + `reshape_drain_epochs()`.

**Terminal coast and the flip.** The parent chain runs to its terminal block `B` — the first block whose parent QC lands past the cut, since no block before it can know it is the last — then *coasts*: empty fallback blocks that exist only to certify `B` and hand off. The coast lasts until the parent's committed chain carries `B`'s commit proof, a round-contiguous pair at or above `B`; from there the parent's proposals address the head, which no longer seats it, and it certifies nothing further. The header of the terminal-epoch chain carries `SplitChildRoots`, the two child subtree roots, which every replica checks compose to the parent's state root: `hash_internal(left, right) == parent_root` (INV-RESHAPE-3).

Each child's genesis block is then derived, by every seated member independently, from frozen content only. Both inputs are in the chain the members are already following. The subtree root is the terminal's `SplitChildRoots` entry, self-verifying by the composition check above. The clock is the canonical certificate over the terminal — carried as the `parent_qc` of the terminal's committed successor, the next block the follow accepts — and **never** the QC served alongside the terminal, which may be a higher-round re-certification from the coast whose stamp is a gossip artifact rather than consensus content (INV-RESHAPE-2/4).

Members flip on that derivation at the cut, not an epoch later when the beacon publishes the child's anchor (INV-RESHAPE-11). The trigger is a terminal the member has **commit-proven**: two QCs can exist at one height, two commits cannot, so a follower that is not a member of the parent's committee verifies the two-chain against the parent's resolved committee rather than trusting a single certificate. A parent half — an existing member crossing to a child — runs the same walk over its own chain with every write skipped, since it seeds the child by cloning state it already holds. A member that cannot prove commitment keeps following and falls back to the anchor path, which is also what a late joiner takes. Adoption fails closed unless the store holds exactly the subtree the genesis names.

Children seat, begin proposing at `terminal.height + 1`, and report their own boundary crossings to the beacon; the parent's committee stays seated, serving `B`, and dissolves once the beacon observes both children live. The beacon's anchor still seeds third parties and joiners, and a pre-staffed child compares it against the genesis it derived when it lands — a disagreement means the local parent chain and the network disagree about a committed block, so the child re-seeds from the attested anchor rather than diverging silently.

**Retention for stragglers.** The parent's terminal boundary record is retained in beacon state until the beacon observes both children live past genesis, and thereafter for `TERMINAL_EVIDENCE_EPOCHS` windows past that handoff epoch, so late joiners and counterpart shards can still resolve the terminal anchor, its committee, and the settled-transaction root (INV-BEACON-8). The span is counted in epochs because the evidence it holds arrives on the beacon's own cadence: a terminal block's parent QC lands past the cut, so it cannot exist until the window after the one it closes, and the beacon block for that window is composed while the crossing is still being produced — the contribution folds a window later again. A span in transaction-artifact units is shorter than one epoch by construction, and would expire the record before the root it exists to project ever lands on it. The schedule window the record is read through keys on the cut for the same reason: the shard leaves the trie there, two windows before its contribution folds.

## 3. The merge lifecycle

Merging is the split run backwards, with one asymmetry: the successors already exist and are running.

**Pairing and keeper draw.** Both children must assert merge triggers within the TTL window. On pairing, the beacon draws a **keeper committee** — half from each child's ready members, seeded from beacon randomness. Keepers keep running their own child (status unchanged) and take on a second duty: building the merged parent store. Keepers are **pinned** — the shuffle skips them, as it skips all mid-reshape shards, because a rotated-out keeper would strand the merged committee below quorum (INV-RESHAPE-7).

**Preparation.** Each keeper snap-syncs the *sibling* child's subtree alongside its own and emits `ReshapeReady` when the merged store is assembled. The merged store is a composition, not a copy: two child subtrees under one root, verified as `hash_internal(left_root, right_root)`.

**Gate, drain and execution.** When ready keepers reach 2f+1 of the merged committee target, the fold latches the merge's drain exactly as it does a split's: both children read as reshaping from the window that shows it, and each asserts quiet under the merge's admission into its own half of the record. The draining view is keyed from the record rather than from the keepers, so a child whose keepers have all dropped still reads as draining. Only once both halves carry a quiet bit does the fold stamp one cut onto both children — a merge pairs them irrevocably in a single step — under the same window of lead a split gets; a merge whose children do not both quiet by `drain_by` cancels like a split. The merge's pairing aborts both children's rotations, so a merge that cycles through latch and cancel holds its children unrotated. The fold at that cut then executes: both children's lookahead entries are replaced by the parent's (committee = exactly the keepers), non-keepers return to the pool, and the parent gets a placeholder boundary.

**Composition and the flip.** Both children coast to their terminal blocks, and each keeper walks both chains to recognise them — one is the child it already runs, the other the sibling it synced. Every input to the merged genesis is in that pair. The state root is `hash_internal(r_left, r_right)` over the two terminal roots, each attested by its own chain, so the pair cannot name a subtree neither terminal committed. The clock is the cut instant itself: the end of the window the children terminate at, which the keepers and the beacon fold read from the same schedule.

The clock cannot come from the children's terminal certificates, and this is the asymmetry with a split. A split child's clock has a unique in-chain source — the terminal's committed successor carries exactly one `parent_qc` over it. A merging child has no such successor to hand the parent: the beacon sees only the *canonical boundary QC*, the highest-ranked entry across the committed proposal set, which is an outcome of the beacon's own consensus and so unpredictable to any keeper. Anchoring at the scheduled cut removes the dependence.

Keepers derive the genesis from both terminals and the cut, adopt it against the union they assembled, and seat the merged committee; the children dissolve. A reformed parent that flips at the cut goes on to fold its own first boundary crossing, so it anchors itself from real production rather than waiting for the beacon to compose one from its children's terminal contributions; the compose remains as the path for a parent whose keepers did not flip early. Each child's terminal boundary is retained until the parent is established, mirroring the split retention rule.

## 4. Straddler settlement: atomicity across a vanishing shard

The straddler problem: a cross-shard transaction is mid-flight — provisioned, maybe executed, not yet finalized — when one of its participating shards `P` reaches its terminal block and stops existing. The counterpart shard `Q` holds locks and awaits `P`'s verdict. Without a rule, two failure modes open, and both were the motivating incidents for this design:

- **H1 (liveness):** `P` dies without delivering; `Q` holds locks forever.
- **H2 (safety):** `P`'s EC is aggregatable from held vote shares *after* termination — `Q` could finalize a result that `P`'s chain never applied, a one-sided commit.

The rejected fix was *inheritance*: let `P`'s children adopt and complete the parent's in-flight work. It fails for a fundamental reason. Whether an EC "exists" is a gossip-time fact — a leader holding vote shares can materialize one arbitrarily late — so different replicas (and the two children) can legitimately disagree on it. Any rule keyed on EC existence turns atomicity into a race. **The only admissible inputs are frozen chain content.** That principle produces the actual design: a core member reads its siblings' verdicts from their committed state ([04-atomic-commitment.md](04-atomic-commitment.md) §3), and a certificate decides nothing on another shard, so H2 has no input to act on.

**No cut falls under a yes.** `P`'s cut is scheduled only once `P` has asserted, under its draining admission, that no row of it hears (§2): no shared row in flight, no stated row and no accepted row. A stated row of `P` leaves only by accepting or by an abort on a sibling's leaving, and an accepted row only once every sibling has accepted or left, so when `P` is quiet every row it shared with a survivor `Q` either never stated, was refused, or accepted and ended after `Q` had accepted. A survivor therefore reads a departed `P` from its own row's entry, and needs nothing of `P`'s terminal: once its window no longer runs `P`, an accepted entry accepts and anything else refuses, and that reading is true. Intake pauses from the latch, toward and from `P`, and a pending core member beside `P` is not run, so `P` gains no shared row in flight while it drains and a quiet `P` stays quiet up to its cut. `P`'s terminal then ends every accepted row it still holds by an `End` line and fates every other row; a row there that hears is only the recovery residual of INV-EXEC-1, which the terminal reports as an error and in the `terminal_fated_hearing_rows` metric (INV-RESHAPE-5).

**The settled set.** For a terminated shard `P`, define `S_P` as the set of cross-shard transactions a finalization committed in `P`'s chain at or before the terminal block `B` settled — a core member's accept among them — across the window reaching back to the terminating reshape's admission (backed off by `RETENTION_HORIZON`). The window must reach admission, not a fixed span behind the terminal: the fence below holds straddlers from the moment the reshape is admitted, and a scheduled window spans multiple epochs, so a terminal-relative span would silently drop settlements made early in it and turn their absence from `S_P` into a false "never settled". `S_P` is a pure function of `P`'s frozen chain: each block's certificate root enumerates the finalizations it carries, and each of those names the transactions it settles. `P`'s terminal header alone carries `S_P`'s merkle root (`terminal_settled_txs`); the beacon fold records that root into `P`'s terminal `ShardBoundary` and projects it onto `P`'s `ShardAnchor` in the topology snapshot every survivor folds. The set is thus **beacon-attested**: a survivor knows the root from its own beacon fold before it fetches a single byte (INV-RESHAPE-6).

**Acquisition.** A survivor that needs the actual set fetches it (`SettledTxsFetch`, keyed by the terminal's `TerminalEvidence`): the wanted set is re-derived on every beacon fold, each request asks `P`'s terminal committee for the window's transaction list, an answer is accepted only if `settled_txs_root_from_hashes(fetched) == attested_root`, peers rotate on failure, and a shard drops out of the wanted set once its terminal evidence expires — the same instant the fence stops accepting an answer built from it.

**The fence** (a consensus rule, enforced at vote): an abandonment record naming a transaction party to a departed shard `P` is votable if and only if `S_P` names none of its transactions; a voter that has not acquired `S_P` defers, and the record is proposable only inside the window the set can be read in. Absence from a complete, beacon-attested set is proof rather than ignorance, so a record never aborts what `P` settled.

**The boundary record.** The set stops being readable at the terminal-evidence window, and a straddler can outlive it. While the set is still readable, a survivor commits to *its own* chain a record naming, for the transactions its account still holds party to `P`, which of them `P` left unsettled. Scope is the survivor's outstanding set — bounded by its own drain, not by `P`'s traffic. It is proposed inside the window every running replica can check it in; one that cannot resolve the set withholds its vote rather than checking, so forging a record takes a quorum, not a proposer. Once committed it is ordinary history, read off each replica's own chain at any distance — including a replica that was switched off for the whole of `P`'s readable window. What a record covers is then decided by the record, so the window's expiry stops deciding it.

**Abandonment is an attested outcome.** A transaction can be committed and never settled: a counterpart terminates under it, the fence rejects a certificate that arrives after the cut, or more than `f` replicas restart together and strand a provisioned batch. Each shard holds a member row for every transaction it has committed and not resolved — written by the committing block, removed by whichever committed block settles it, and so committed state that survives a restart leaving no execution state behind. The first block anchored past a transaction's own deadline names it in its tick manifest as an `Aborted` member, alongside whatever else that tick runs, on a tick that needs no surviving execution state behind it; a leg past its deadline is not named. What a leg issued its producer's commit fold settles: retired on its consumer's `Taken`, or taken back on its consumer's `Never`, which charges nothing and deletes no row, and the leg entry closes on those removals. A leg that never ran keeps its row, with its fee hold and its place in the drain, until the block anchored at the close of the leg entry window (the deadline plus `CLAIM_WINDOW`) fates it, which is the one charge of a leg that never ran. The line carries all its abandonment settles — the shards its committing block placed it on, the share and the charge that block priced, its deadline, and the crossings it answers `Never` for — so a replica holding nothing of the transaction but the block seats the abandonment its peers seat and builds the receipt they build. A tick seats at most `MAX_TXS_PER_BLOCK` members, the outcomes one execution vote carries, and every one of them is a member its manifest names: `MAX_NAMED_MEMBERS_PER_TICK` is that same figure.

The deadline is the transaction's own: its `validity_range` end — the last block that could have included it anywhere — plus the longest a cross-shard transaction can take to finalize. Both figures are chain content on every replica, which is what lets a committee sign a verdict about them rather than each node reporting its own; and because the deadline is the point past which inclusion was already impossible anywhere, an abandonment never discards work that is merely slow. `P`'s own chain settles its side at the terminal block, which names an `End` line for every accepted row and a fate — an abort no certificate attests, deleting the row and burning its charge — for every other member row it still holds; under the quiet gate none of those rows hears. A fate is not the terminal's alone: a survivor's unstated member, once every shard it reaches is unreadable, is let go on an ordinary block by the discard of the tick holding it and a fate beside it. A clock fates only a member whose certificate is owed nowhere: a leg still waiting, and a member settled on this shard's word alone that a discard let go of, each at the close of its leg entry window. A shared member in flight that has not stated is aborted at its stating window's close, unless its tick still owes its determined half. A stated member has no clock: it leaves only by accepting or by an abort on a sibling's leaving, since a sibling may already have accepted on its yes, and no deadline, record, terminal or recovery ends it. A recovery's discard releases only rows in flight; a member it released that ran and shares its verdict is aborted at once, having never stated. Its row keeps the crossings it consumes through its run, and an abort no record covers answers `Never` for each of them, in the unseen form a replica builds from the line alone; the producer takes its record back on that `Never` as on any other. A sibling reads that exit and refuses. A committed record naming a transaction unsettled is what lets an abort past its deadline release the tick holding a member in flight. The fence asks a settled set nothing about an abandonment line: a line aborts a member that ever ran only where a record covers it, where it reaches no other shard, where it never stated by its stating window's close, where a sibling's state refuses it, or once a recovery has released it in flight. That the counterpart *departed* licenses nothing on its own: a shard can accept and then leave, and the survivor's own reading of that accept is the evidence, never the departure.

Two things follow. A chain observer reads an abandonment off a certificate exactly as it reads a settlement, single-shard and cross-shard alike, rather than inferring it from a node's own memory. And the reservation the transaction took against its block's work budget is released by that certificate like any other, so a shard that strands work returns to its full admission ceiling instead of losing it permanently.

A shard stops taking core transactions before its cut, and pays for it. From the first window that shows its drain latched until the cut, core intake toward and from it pauses, for the drain span and the folds around it when nothing is at fault. Transactions that touch it alone, and core transactions that do not reach it, run throughout. A row in flight there when intake paused costs its sender a resubmission and its abort charge if it does not state in time, and while any of its rows hears a sibling that cannot be read — halted until its recovery, or behind a reading partition — the reshape waits, and cancels at its drain deadline rather than fall under that yes. That pause is the price of atomic commitment across a cut ([04-atomic-commitment.md](04-atomic-commitment.md) §8).

The net guarantee: **a core transaction finalizes-accept on the survivor if and only if its half applied on the terminated shard's chain at or before the terminal block**, outside the one stated residual of [08-invariants.md](08-invariants.md) INV-EXEC-1: a recovery whose recovered chain lacks a commit-proven block a sibling read. Both sides' verdicts are functions of committed content, so they cannot disagree — atomicity (INV-EXEC-1) survives the death of a participant.

## 5. The orchestrator: one duty machine, both harnesses

The sequencing above — when to open a store, when to snap-sync, when to re-assert readiness, when to fetch terminals, when to adopt and seat — is driven by a single sans-io `ReshapeOrchestrator` (`crates/node`), shared verbatim by the production supervisor and the simulator. It reads a `ReshapeView` — the host's `TopologySchedule`, whose head projects pending reshapes, boundaries and committees and whose retained windows classify a followed block — and steps per-duty state machines:

- **Observer** (split cohort member): open the store, snap-sync the child span, tail the parent while applying child-prefix writes, recognise and commit-prove the terminal crossing as it passes, derive and adopt the genesis, seat.
- **Parent-half** (existing member crossing to a child): walk the local parent chain for the same crossing, applying nothing, then seed the child store by cloning the parent through the child's genesis height, adopt, seat.
- **Keeper** (merge): re-assert ready, walk both children for their terminals, assemble the merged store from both spans, derive and adopt the composed genesis, seat.

Each duty keeps an anchor-driven fallback for the case its walk cannot resolve — a late-discovered duty, a fetch gap, or a predecessor that has already dissolved.

All gate predicates are pure functions over committed state (for example, "children seeded" means both child boundaries have non-zero block hashes), so production and simulation provably make the same decisions from the same facts. The orchestrator exists precisely because three hand-written copies of this logic had drifted apart.

Supporting safety and liveness machinery, briefly (details in [05-byzantine-safety.md](05-byzantine-safety.md) and [03-state-and-sync.md](03-state-and-sync.md)): the shuffle skips mid-reshape shards entirely, and a split cancels any rotation already in flight on its target rather than carve a not-yet-ready entrant into a child's parent half; `top_up_committees` refills any committee left under strength by a reshape-depleted pool, from what the recovery reserve leaves; store-lock arbitration keeps the host supervisor from wiping a store a reshape duty is building in; and remote-header routing is terminal-clamped so drained shards' terminal artifacts stay fetchable through the retention window.
