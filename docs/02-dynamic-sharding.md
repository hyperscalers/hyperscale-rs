# Dynamic sharding: splits, merges, and straddler settlement

Hyperscale's shard topology is elastic. A shard whose state grows past a threshold **splits** into two children; two sibling shards whose combined state shrinks below a threshold **merge** back into their parent. Both happen at runtime, orchestrated by the beacon, without halting the network — and without breaking atomicity for cross-shard transactions in flight across the boundary.

Three structural choices make this tractable, and everything else in this document is machinery built on them:

1. **A shard is a subtree.** State lives in one global binary JMT; a shard is a prefix subtree of it ([03-state-and-sync.md](03-state-and-sync.md)). A split hands each child one of the parent root's two children; a merge composes the parent root back as `hash_internal(left, right)`. Resharding is a tree operation with a one-hash continuity proof, not a data migration.
2. **The boundary is a block, not a negotiation.** A reshaping shard terminates at a definite **terminal block**, fixed by epoch schedule. The system never waits for in-flight work to drain; that would make the boundary timing-dependent and non-deterministic. Instead, transactions straddling the boundary are settled after the fact by rules over frozen chain content (§4).
3. **Everything derives from committed facts.** Cohort draws are seeded by beacon randomness; readiness is witnessed on-chain; successor genesis blocks are pure functions of the predecessor's terminal block and the scheduled cut, and fail closed when the store does not hold the subtree the genesis names. No step depends on message timing or operator input.

Main code homes: reshape admission/gating in `crates/beacon` (the fold's reshape module, over `PendingReshape` in `crates/types`), the flip and duty machinery in `crates/node` (`ReshapeOrchestrator`, split/merge flip, observer/keeper bootstrap), settlement in the `crates/types` settled-set predicate, fetched and served from `crates/node`, and checked by the vote fence in `crates/shard` and the finalize gate in `crates/execution`, thresholds in `crates/types` (`ReshapeThresholds`).

## 1. Triggers: load-driven, proof-carrying

A shard proposes its own resharding. When a shard's committed substate byte total crosses `ReshapeThresholds::split_bytes`, or the beacon's mean of its block fullness crosses `split_fullness`, its proposer includes a `ReshapeTrigger::Split` assertion in the block manifest. The assertion is a *claim about committed state*, and every replica validates it against its own byte accounting before voting — a Byzantine proposer cannot conjure a split. Merge triggers work the same way against much lower thresholds on both measures (`merge_bytes` and `merge_fullness`, each derived from its split threshold); the wide hysteresis gap between a fresh split child's size and the merge threshold prevents split/merge oscillation. Reshaping is disabled unless the thresholds are explicitly configured. Governance can move them by on-chain parameter vote, which is also how operationally driven merges are effected.

One place the predicate is deliberately out of play: across a pending halt recovery, a block whose ancestry crosses the sync-admitted halted suffix carries no assertion, and no replica requires one. The suffix is QC-attested but never locally executed by the fresh committee, so its byte total is unknowable until it commits — a commit that needs the very blocks being gated. Every member of the only committee that can vote there synced the same suffix, so the omission is agreed on both sides. Once the suffix commits and the frontier reconciles from storage, ordinary accounting resumes; an over-threshold shard simply asserts on a later block.

Triggers must be re-asserted; a trigger silent for `RESHAPE_TRIGGER_TTL_EPOCHS` lapses. The beacon tracks each shard's pending reshape in a `PendingReshape` record — `Split { cohort, cohort_seed, ... }` or `Merge { halves, keepers, ... }` — inside `BeaconState`.

## 2. The split lifecycle

**Admission and cohort draw.** On folding a split trigger (gated on the free validator pool being deep enough to staff it), the beacon draws a committee-sized **observer cohort** from the pool, seeded by `cohort_seed` — beacon randomness frozen at admission. The shuffled cohort is halved: the first half is assigned to the left child, the remainder to the right. Observers get status `Observing` and start work immediately. Because the seed is frozen, a lapsed-then-reasserted split re-derives the *identical* cohort — an observer's sync target never moves under it.

**Preparation (make-before-break).** While the parent keeps producing blocks, each observer snap-syncs its assigned child's subtree: a verified range-fetch of the child's key span against the beacon-anchored state root, followed by tailing the parent chain and applying each new block's child-prefix writes ([03-state-and-sync.md](03-state-and-sync.md) §5). When caught up, the observer emits a `ReshapeReady` witness — an on-chain, merkle-proven fact. The observer keeps following after that, because the follow is also how it will recognise the parent's terminal crossing as it passes and take over from it.

**The readiness gate.** The beacon's fold schedules the split only when **both** children can seat a live committee: each child's prospective membership — its half of the parent's current committee (assigned deterministically from beacon randomness) plus its ready cohort seats — must reach 2f+1 of the committee target (INV-RESHAPE-1). Parent members carry their readiness and placement epoch across the flip; losing placement history was an observed liveness bug — it strands committees. Until both halves pass, nothing changes. If readiness doesn't arrive within `RESHAPE_READY_TTL_EPOCHS`, the reshape is abandoned and the cohort returns to the pool.

**Scheduling the cut.** The first passing fold does not reshape the trie. It stamps a cut — always on a *later* window than the one it opens (INV-RESHAPE-9) — onto the retained reshape record and onto the parent's boundary record, and stops there. The lead is what makes the cut knowable in advance: a proposer decides whether its header must carry the terminal-window verdicts by reading its own window's promotion-frozen projection, and a cut named for the window that fold opens would arrive after the proposers who needed it. One window is the minimum that suffices; more would only add latency.

Because the cut is published to proposers the moment it is stamped, it cannot be taken back (INV-RESHAPE-10). The readiness TTL governs admission-to-scheduling and stops there; a cohort that degrades afterwards surfaces as a stalled handoff, not a retracted cut. The carve the gate approved is frozen onto the record with it, since there is no second gate to approve a different one.

**Execution.** The fold *at* the cut consumes the record: the parent's lookahead committee entry is replaced by the two children's committees, and the children receive placeholder boundary records (zero block hash) so they cannot yet be mistaken for snap-sync anchors. A member jailed or withdrawn since the carve was frozen is dropped rather than moved, and the child seats short until `top_up_committees` backfills it in the same fold.

**Terminal coast and the flip.** The parent chain runs to its terminal block `B` — the first block whose parent QC lands past the cut, since no block before it can know it is the last — then *coasts*: empty fallback blocks that exist only to certify `B` and hand off. The header of the terminal-epoch chain carries `SplitChildRoots`, the two child subtree roots, which every replica checks compose to the parent's state root: `hash_internal(left, right) == parent_root` (INV-RESHAPE-3).

Each child's genesis block is then derived, by every seated member independently, from frozen content only. Both inputs are in the chain the members are already following. The subtree root is the terminal's `SplitChildRoots` entry, self-verifying by the composition check above. The clock is the canonical certificate over the terminal — carried as the `parent_qc` of the terminal's committed successor, the next block the follow accepts — and **never** the QC served alongside the terminal, which may be a higher-round re-certification from the coast whose stamp is a gossip artifact rather than consensus content (INV-RESHAPE-2/4).

Members flip on that derivation at the cut, not an epoch later when the beacon publishes the child's anchor (INV-RESHAPE-11). The trigger is a terminal the member has **commit-proven**: two QCs can exist at one height, two commits cannot, so a follower that is not a member of the parent's committee verifies the two-chain against the parent's resolved committee rather than trusting a single certificate. A parent half — an existing member crossing to a child — runs the same walk over its own chain with every write skipped, since it seeds the child by cloning state it already holds. A member that cannot prove commitment keeps following and falls back to the anchor path, which is also what a late joiner takes. Adoption fails closed unless the store holds exactly the subtree the genesis names.

Children seat, begin proposing at `terminal.height + 1`, and report their own boundary crossings to the beacon; the parent dissolves once the beacon observes both children live. The beacon's anchor still seeds third parties and joiners, and a pre-staffed child compares it against the genesis it derived when it lands — a disagreement means the local parent chain and the network disagree about a committed block, so the child re-seeds from the attested anchor rather than diverging silently.

**Retention for stragglers.** The parent's terminal boundary record is retained in beacon state until the beacon observes both children live past genesis, and thereafter for `TERMINAL_EVIDENCE_EPOCHS` windows past that handoff epoch, so late joiners and counterpart shards can still resolve the terminal anchor, its committee, and the settled-transaction root (INV-BEACON-8). The span is counted in epochs because the evidence it holds arrives on the beacon's own cadence: a terminal block's parent QC lands past the cut, so it cannot exist until the window after the one it closes, and the beacon block for that window is composed while the crossing is still being produced — the contribution folds a window later again. A span in transaction-artifact units is shorter than one epoch by construction, and would expire the record before the root it exists to project ever lands on it. The schedule window the record is read through keys on the cut for the same reason: the shard leaves the trie there, two windows before its contribution folds.

## 3. The merge lifecycle

Merging is the split run backwards, with one asymmetry: the successors already exist and are running.

**Pairing and keeper draw.** Both children must assert merge triggers within the TTL window. On pairing, the beacon draws a **keeper committee** — half from each child's ready members, seeded from beacon randomness. Keepers keep running their own child (status unchanged) and take on a second duty: building the merged parent store. Keepers are **pinned** — the shuffle skips them, as it skips all mid-reshape shards, because a rotated-out keeper would strand the merged committee below quorum (INV-RESHAPE-7).

**Preparation.** Each keeper snap-syncs the *sibling* child's subtree alongside its own and emits `ReshapeReady` when the merged store is assembled. The merged store is a composition, not a copy: two child subtrees under one root, verified as `hash_internal(left_root, right_root)`.

**Gate and execution.** When ready keepers reach 2f+1 of the merged committee target, the fold stamps one cut onto both children — a merge pairs them irrevocably in a single step — under the same window of lead a split gets. The fold at that cut then executes: both children's lookahead entries are replaced by the parent's (committee = exactly the keepers), non-keepers return to the pool, and the parent gets a placeholder boundary.

**Composition and the flip.** Both children coast to their terminal blocks, and each keeper walks both chains to recognise them — one is the child it already runs, the other the sibling it synced. Every input to the merged genesis is in that pair. The state root is `hash_internal(r_left, r_right)` over the two terminal roots, each attested by its own chain, so the pair cannot name a subtree neither terminal committed. The clock is the cut instant itself: the end of the window the children terminate at, which the keepers and the beacon fold read from the same schedule.

The clock cannot come from the children's terminal certificates, and this is the asymmetry with a split. A split child's clock has a unique in-chain source — the terminal's committed successor carries exactly one `parent_qc` over it. A merging child has no such successor to hand the parent: the beacon sees only the *canonical boundary QC*, the highest-ranked entry across the committed proposal set, which is an outcome of the beacon's own consensus and so unpredictable to any keeper. Anchoring at the scheduled cut removes the dependence.

Keepers derive the genesis from both terminals and the cut, adopt it against the union they assembled, and seat the merged committee; the children dissolve. A reformed parent that flips at the cut goes on to fold its own first boundary crossing, so it anchors itself from real production rather than waiting for the beacon to compose one from its children's terminal contributions; the compose remains as the path for a parent whose keepers did not flip early. Each child's terminal boundary is retained until the parent is established, mirroring the split retention rule.

## 4. Straddler settlement: atomicity across a vanishing shard

The straddler problem: a cross-shard transaction is mid-flight — provisioned, maybe executed, not yet finalized — when one of its participating shards `P` reaches its terminal block and stops existing. The counterpart shard `Q` holds locks and awaits `P`'s execution certificate. Without a rule, two failure modes open, and both were the motivating incidents for this design:

- **H1 (liveness):** `P` dies without delivering; `Q` holds locks forever.
- **H2 (safety):** `P`'s EC is aggregatable from held vote shares *after* termination — `Q` could finalize a result that `P`'s chain never applied, a one-sided commit.

The rejected fix was *inheritance*: let `P`'s children adopt and complete the parent's in-flight work. It fails for a fundamental reason. Whether an EC "exists" is a gossip-time fact — a leader holding vote shares can materialize one arbitrarily late — so different replicas (and the two children) can legitimately disagree on it. Any rule keyed on EC existence turns atomicity into a race. **The only admissible inputs are frozen chain content.** That principle produces the actual design.

**The settled set.** For a terminated shard `P`, define `S_P` as the set of cross-shard transactions whose certificate committed in `P`'s chain at or before the terminal block `B`, across the window reaching back to the terminating reshape's admission (backed off by `RETENTION_HORIZON`). The window must reach admission, not a fixed span behind the terminal: the fence below holds straddlers from the moment the reshape is admitted, and a scheduled window spans multiple epochs, so a terminal-relative span would silently drop settlements made early in it and turn their absence from `S_P` into a false "never settled". `S_P` is a pure function of `P`'s frozen chain: each block's certificate root enumerates the finalizations it carries, and each of those names the transactions it settles. `P`'s terminal header alone carries `S_P`'s merkle root (`terminal_settled_txs`); the beacon fold records that root into `P`'s terminal `ShardBoundary` and projects it onto `P`'s `ShardAnchor` in the topology snapshot every survivor folds. The set is thus **beacon-attested**: a survivor knows the root from its own beacon fold before it fetches a single byte (INV-RESHAPE-6).

**Acquisition.** A survivor that needs the actual set fetches it (`SettledTxsFetch`, keyed by the terminal's `TerminalEvidence`): the wanted set is re-derived on every beacon fold, each request asks `P`'s terminal committee for the window's transaction list, an answer is accepted only if `settled_txs_root_from_hashes(fetched) == attested_root`, peers rotate on failure, and a shard drops out of the wanted set once its terminal evidence expires — the same instant the fence stops accepting an answer built from it.

**The fence** (a consensus rule, enforced at vote and at local finalization): a block carrying a `Finalization` whose certificate names a shard `P` that has terminated — *or is scheduled to* — is votable if and only if every transaction that certificate attests is in `S_P`. The fence engages the moment `P` has an admitted terminating reshape, not only once `P` is past-terminal. A survivor that finalized a straddler in the window between the reshape's admission and `P`'s terminal block could otherwise apply a transaction `P` never settled: `P` produces its own EC yet, under an asymmetric partition, never receives the survivor's before its cut. The verdicts for an outcome naming `P` follow: while `P` is scheduled-terminal and the settled set does not yet exist, it defers; once `P` is past-terminal and the transaction is in `S_P`, it passes; once the set is acquired and the transaction is absent, it is rejected; past the terminal-evidence window, it is rejected absolutely. Deferral resolves only on evidence — the settled set arriving, the reshape lapsing, or the schedule evicting `P` — never on a local clock: a deadline abort could contradict a settlement `P` already committed, and the terminal-evidence window guarantees termination everywhere, not agreement. Symmetric checks gate a replica's own finalization path, so an honest node never produces what the fence would refuse (INV-RESHAPE-5). The scheduled-terminal check keys on frozen schedule content (the active and lookahead windows' pending-reshape projection), so both sides derive the same verdict.

**The boundary record.** The set stops being readable at the terminal-evidence window, and a straddler can outlive it. While the set is still readable, a survivor commits to *its own* chain a record naming, for the transactions its account still holds party to `P`, which of them `P` left unsettled. Scope is the survivor's outstanding set — bounded by its own drain, not by `P`'s traffic. It is proposed inside the window every running replica can check it in; one that cannot resolve the set withholds its vote rather than checking, so forging a record takes a quorum, not a proposer. Once committed it is ordinary history, read off each replica's own chain at any distance — including a replica that was switched off for the whole of `P`'s readable window. The fence asks nothing about a transaction a record covers, so the window's expiry stops deciding it.

**Abandonment is an attested outcome.** A transaction can be committed and never settled: a counterpart terminates under it, the fence rejects a certificate that arrives after the cut, or more than `f` replicas restart together and strand a provisioned batch. Each shard holds a member row for every transaction it has committed and not resolved — written by the committing block, removed by whichever committed block settles it, and so committed state that survives a restart leaving no execution state behind. The first block anchored past a transaction's own deadline names it in its tick manifest as an `Aborted` member, alongside whatever else that tick runs, on a tick that needs no surviving execution state behind it; a leg past its deadline is not named, and its reclaim resolves it.

The deadline is the transaction's own: its `validity_range` end — the last block that could have included it anywhere — plus the longest a cross-shard transaction can take to finalize. Both figures are chain content on every replica, which is what lets a committee sign a verdict about them rather than each node reporting its own; and because the deadline is the point past which inclusion was already impossible anywhere, an abandonment never discards work that is merely slow. `P`'s own chain settles its side at the terminal block, which names a fate — an abort — for every member row it still holds, and the fence on every survivor decides the straddlers from `S_P`. A committed record naming a transaction unsettled is what lets an abort past its deadline release the tick holding it, and one the set named as settled is never abandoned. That the counterpart *departed* licenses nothing on its own: a shard can settle its half and then leave, and composing an abandonment discards the tick that would have settled it — so what spends a tick has to be evidence the fence cannot contradict, which is the record and not the departure.

Two things follow. A chain observer reads an abandonment off a certificate exactly as it reads a settlement, single-shard and cross-shard alike, rather than inferring it from a node's own memory. And the reservation the transaction took against its block's work budget is released by that certificate like any other, so a shard that strands work returns to its full admission ceiling instead of losing it permanently.

A shard therefore selects transactions right up to its terminal cut. There is no margin before it in which selection stops: a straddler costs its sender a resubmission and nothing else — settlement is what writes receipts, so an abandoned transaction burns no fee and moves no value — while a margin wide enough to cover a settlement round stops the shard entirely for that whole span, on every reshape, whether or not anything was in flight.

The net guarantee: **a cross-shard transaction finalizes-accept on the survivor if and only if its half applied on the terminated shard's chain at or before the terminal block.** Both sides' verdicts are functions of frozen content, so they cannot disagree — atomicity (INV-EXEC-1) survives the death of a participant.

## 5. The orchestrator: one duty machine, both harnesses

The sequencing above — when to open a store, when to snap-sync, when to re-assert readiness, when to fetch terminals, when to adopt and seat — is driven by a single sans-io `ReshapeOrchestrator` (`crates/node`), shared verbatim by the production supervisor and the simulator. It reads a `ReshapeView` — the host's `TopologySchedule`, whose head projects pending reshapes, boundaries and committees and whose retained windows classify a followed block — and steps per-duty state machines:

- **Observer** (split cohort member): open the store, snap-sync the child span, tail the parent while applying child-prefix writes, recognise and commit-prove the terminal crossing as it passes, derive and adopt the genesis, seat.
- **Parent-half** (existing member crossing to a child): walk the local parent chain for the same crossing, applying nothing, then seed the child store by cloning the parent through the child's genesis height, adopt, seat.
- **Keeper** (merge): re-assert ready, walk both children for their terminals, assemble the merged store from both spans, derive and adopt the composed genesis, seat.

Each duty keeps an anchor-driven fallback for the case its walk cannot resolve — a late-discovered duty, a fetch gap, or a predecessor that has already dissolved.

All gate predicates are pure functions over committed state (for example, "children seeded" means both child boundaries have non-zero block hashes), so production and simulation provably make the same decisions from the same facts. The orchestrator exists precisely because three hand-written copies of this logic had drifted apart.

Supporting safety and liveness machinery, briefly (details in [05-byzantine-safety.md](05-byzantine-safety.md) and [03-state-and-sync.md](03-state-and-sync.md)): the shuffle skips mid-reshape shards entirely, and a split cancels any rotation already in flight on its target rather than carve a not-yet-ready entrant into a child's parent half; `top_up_committees` refills any committee left under strength by a reshape-depleted pool; store-lock arbitration keeps the host supervisor from wiping a store a reshape duty is building in; and remote-header routing is terminal-clamped so drained shards' terminal artifacts stay fetchable through the retention window.
